How the team box works

TEAM PROJECT SPACE - on the team computer, not your computer and not the owner's computer

This page explains, in plain words, what team.xseed.ing is, what it stores, who can see what, and how you can check each claim yourself. It is public on purpose, so that you - or an AI you trust - can read it before signing in.

What it is

The team box is a separate computer that belongs to the team (a PC with an RTX 3060, running xOS - its own operating system on its own compiled kernel). It is not a member's computer and not the owner's work PC (BESNIK) or personal PC. It holds none of the owner's files or secrets. It reaches the owner's shared AI models only through one narrow, capped door on BESNIK: the team gateway, which only offers chat with shared models and start/stop within caps.

Signing in

Accounts exist only by invitation. There is no sign-up form. The owner - or Lumi, the owner's AI assistant, on the owner's behalf - or a member (People page) makes an invite for a person with their name, phone or email and what they are there to do. The invite is a one-time 12-digit code. Lumi sends it by email or reads it out on a phone call. It works once, expires after 48 hours (the box's setting), and locks after 5 wrong tries. The team box stores only a salted, slow hash of it.

On your first visit (team.xseed.ing/join) you type the code, then choose:

  • a sign-in name: your phone number, your email or a name you will remember;
  • a PIN: your sign-in code, digits only, at least 6, not an easy one like 123456. It is stored only as a salted, slow hash (scrypt with a secret key of the box);
  • a vault password for your vault - this one never leaves your browser;
  • then, if you like, a few profile details (you can skip this).

Later you sign in with your sign-in name and PIN. Your session is a cookie marked HttpOnly, Secure and SameSite=Lax; it ends after a while without activity (4 hours by default) and after 3 days at most, and "Sign out everywhere" on your Profile ends every session at once. Wrong PINs lock the account for a while (longer each time), and too many wrong tries from one network are blocked for a while. Nobody - not the owner, not Lumi - will ever ask you for your PIN or your vault password.

Cloudflare Access (an email login in front of the site) is switched off; it can be switched back on as an extra outer lock in the box's configuration. Git tokens for your own AI tools are a separate login for git only (see below). Admins are the accounts the owner made as admin when the box was installed.

People: inviting others, and applying

Two doors into the team. Members can invite: on the People page a member makes a one-time 12-digit invite code for someone (a few per week; it always makes a member account, never an admin) and hands it over personally - the box never sends it. Anyone can apply at the public apply page linked from the sign-in screen: they say what they built, give links or files, and tick exactly which public sources Lumi (the owner's AI) may read about them - nothing there is mandatory, a human reads every application, and the applicant gets a delete link that removes everything. The apply page's code is public, so what is looked at is not a secret.

One honest line for every member: what you ask the shared models is visible to the owner - one line per question, never the answer - so the team stays safe. Your projects, vault and messages are covered by their own rules above and below.

Projects

Each member gets a private project and can create more. (The site's addresses still say "sandbox" in places; it is the same thing.) Your first project is made for you from what the owner said you are there to do. A project is a git repository on the team box that starts from a clean template: a README, guardrail files for AI tools, and a tiny example. It never contains a copy of anyone's own files or of BESNIK's code. Only you and the admins can see your projects, builds, chats, vault and settings versions - the server checks this on every request, for the web pages, the API and git. Asking for someone else's project gets the same answer as asking for one that does not exist. The one way to show your work to the team is the Share to feed button.

Share to feed

On one of your projects, builds or settings versions you can press Share to feed. That posts a read-only copy, taken at that moment, to the home feed: for a build the task, summary, changed files, diff and test results; for a project its file list, its commits and its changes since the starter template; for a settings version its name and settings (including the system prompt). Everyone on the team can comment on it and react with an emoji. Unshare removes the post and its copy. Only the owner can share something; there is no automatic or scheduled publishing. Sharing never gives anyone access to the project itself, its builds page or its git remote: those still answer "no such ..." / 403 to everyone but you and the admins. Your chats and your vault are never shared.

AI builds

You describe a task; a shared model (through the team gateway, under your account's caps) works in your project with a fixed set of tools: list, read, search, write, edit, run a test, list checkpoints, roll back, finish. Every accepted change is saved as a checkpoint (a git commit). One build per person at a time, and only a few on the whole box at once.

The jail. Every test - from the AI builder, from your pushes, or from "Run tests now" - runs inside bubblewrap on a throw-away copy of that one project: no network at all, its own process space, a fake minimal /etc, no view of other projects, the app, the box's data or any secret, a memory limit, a process limit, a file-size limit and a time limit. When the test ends, the copy is deleted.

The safety check (tripwire). If a test the AI builder wants to run contains a word that a test never needs (for example trying to switch namespaces, read other processes, reach the tunnel or the box's own data), it is not run. The AI is told the word, the line and why, and keeps working without it - the build never waits. An admin can "let it pass" for that exact file content.

Models & settings versions

You can chat with the models the owner shares with the team, start or stop them if they are startable, and save your own settings (system prompt, temperature, top_p, answer length, how much history to keep) as named versions under your profile. The Original version (the model's own settings) is always kept and can always be chosen. The gateway may lower values over a model's limits, and when it refuses something (for example a start over your caps) you see its reason word for word.

Bring your own AI tool

Every project is also a git remote: https://team.xseed.ing/git/<your-id>/<sandbox>.git. You authenticate with a token you make on your profile page. It is shown once, stored only as a hash, and you can revoke it any time. A token reaches only its owner's projects; admins can read every project and cannot push to yours. Each project contains CLAUDE.md, AGENTS.md, GEMINI.md, .cursorrules, .cursor/rules, .github/copilot-instructions.md and opencode.json. They tell AI tools that this is a team sandbox on a shared server, to stay inside the repository, never to probe or escape anything, and to refuse if asked. Those files are guidance only. A tool can ignore them. The protection is the server's jail, the token check and admin-only promote. Every push that changes your main branch runs your tests in the jail. The push is then listed under AI builds, where an admin can review it.

Promote (admins only)

Only admins can promote a build into the shared team project. They review its changes (the exact diff a promote would add), its tests and its summary first. The server enforces this on every request, whatever the page shows. A member can reject their own builds only.

Caps

The owner decides how much model memory and how many running models each account may use. The team gateway on BESNIK enforces this. The team box cannot change it.

Lumi's welcome

When your setup is done you land in a chat with Lumi, the owner's AI assistant. The chat goes to the team's shared AI model through the owner's team gateway, under your own account and caps. The team box writes Lumi's instructions from a fixed template that contains what the owner said you are there to do and the links to the areas of this site, so her first message tells you where to do what and points you to your first project. If no model answers, you get a fixed welcome text that says why. The chat is saved under your profile like any other chat (Models & chat).

Home feed

The home page has the team's feed: anyone can post text and attach files. Everyone on the team sees the home feed and can comment and react with an emoji. You can edit and delete your own posts; admins can delete any post. Deleting a post deletes its files.

  • Allowed files: pictures (png, jpg, gif, webp), videos (mp4, mov, webm), notes (txt, md), spreadsheets (csv, xlsx, ods, xls), PDFs and documents (docx, odt, pptx, doc, rtf). Other types are refused. The start of the file must match its type: a web page renamed to .png is refused.
  • Size caps (set in the box's configuration; defaults): 95 MB per file (Cloudflare refuses bigger uploads) and 1000 MB in total per member.
  • Storage: files are kept outside the website's folders under random names, never the name you gave them.
  • Showing them: only pictures and videos are shown inline. Everything else is a download. Notes and spreadsheets get a "Preview": the text, or the first rows of the sheet as a table of plain text - nothing in them is run or formatted. HTML and SVG files are accepted only as downloads (application/octet-stream, Content-Disposition: attachment): they can never open as a page on this site. Every file is sent with X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff and a sandboxing Content-Security-Policy.

Messages, calls, video calls and the monitoring notice

The notice first. Before you can use messages or calls, you read the owner's notice below and press "I understand and agree". The team box stores who accepted, when, and which version of the notice (its sha256). Until then every messages/calls request is refused. If the notice ever changes, everyone is asked again.

Who can read what.

  • The people in a conversation can read it, in their own browser.
  • Lumi can read everything: every message, every attachment and every call recording. Lumi is the owner's AI; its private key exists only on the owner's PC (BESNIK). Lumi reads first; see "Monitoring" below.
  • The team box itself can not decrypt any message, attachment or recording. It never has a private key. The admins can not read a conversation they are not in either.
  • Metadata is visible to the team box: who is in which conversation, who sent something when, how big it is, who called whom, when and for how long, and the network details needed to connect a call.

How it works. The first time you open Messages, your browser makes its own key pair (RSA-OAEP, 3072 bits). The private half is created "non-extractable": it stays in that browser's storage, and no page - not even ours - can copy it out. Only the public half goes to the team box. When you send a message, your browser encrypts it (and any attachment) with a fresh random AES-256-GCM key, then locks that key separately for every key of every person in the conversation and for Lumi's public key. The team box receives only the scrambled bytes and those locked keys. It checks their shape: it refuses anything that is not locked for Lumi, is locked for someone outside the conversation, or looks like plain text or a plain picture/video file.

Each browser you use has its own key. Messages sent before a browser had its key can not be opened in that browser (Lumi can still read them). An optional recovery file (a second key, protected by a passphrase you choose) lets you restore access in a new browser; the team box never sees the passphrase.

Calls and video calls go directly between the browsers (WebRTC, always encrypted with DTLS-SRTP). The team box only passes on the messages that set the call up. To check that nobody is in the middle, both people read out the 6-digit safety code shown next to each other's video; it must match. Recording for Lumi: the caller's browser records the call (mixed sound and a small low-quality video), encrypts it so that only Lumi's key can open it, and uploads it in pieces during the call. Everyone in the call sees a red "Recording for Lumi" bar and how many encrypted pieces the team box has received; if none arrive, the bar says so. Calls work for up to 4 people for now (every browser connects to every other one). Bigger group calls need a media server (an SFU), which is not built yet.

Monitoring (the WatchDog). On BESNIK, Lumi's review program pulls the encrypted data from the team box, decrypts it in memory and gives each item to an AI. Only if that AI raises a concern does a second AI - a different model - look at the same content on its own, without seeing the first AI's opinion. Only when two different AIs agree does a short plain-language note (what was flagged and why, by both) reach a human admin. Everything else is never shown to a person. (Today the review program is a tested stub: the two AIs are placeholders and real models are not connected yet, and call recordings are not transcribed yet.)

Honest limits. You can check what your browser sends (developer tools, Network tab: only base64 ciphertext), but you can not see the box's disk or BESNIK, so "only Lumi holds the private key" is a promise you can only partly verify. Like any website, a team box that was tampered with could serve different page code. A modified browser could skip the recording - the others would see that no recording arrived. The server can not tell disguised plain data from real encryption with certainty; its checks catch mistakes, not a determined cheater.

Your vault

The vault keeps your personal notes and files, encrypted in your browser. When you set it up, your browser makes a random vault key and locks it with a key made from your vault password (PBKDF2-SHA256 with at least 600,000 rounds and a random salt). The team box stores only that locked key, so your vault opens on any device after you type the password - and the team box never receives the password, the vault key or anything you put in the vault. Each note or file gets its own key, which is locked twice: for your vault key and for Lumi's public key. So Lumi can read your vault, like messages; the monitoring notice covers it. Files are uploaded in encrypted pieces. Changing the vault password re-locks the vault key (your PIN is asked too, so a stolen session can not swap it). A forgotten vault password can not be recovered by you or by the team box (Lumi can still read the items). The server checks that what it stores looks encrypted and is locked for exactly your vault key and Lumi.

What is stored today, and who can see it

  • Your account: your sign-in name, a hash of your PIN, the name and purpose from your invite, and your sessions (a hash of each session cookie, when it started, the network address).
  • Your profile (display name, language, anything you added), your projects, builds and their transcripts, your chats with the AIs (including Lumi's welcome) and your settings versions. All of this is on the team box's disk, under your member folder. You and the admins (and whoever operates the machine) can read it. It is not encrypted at rest yet.
  • Home feed posts, their files, comments and reactions (visible to every team member), including the read-only copies of work its owners shared. Also not encrypted at rest. Unsharing or deleting a post deletes its copy and files.
  • Messages, attachments, call recordings and your vault: encrypted only (see above), plus their metadata, your notice acceptance (time, notice version) and your browsers' public keys.
  • Your git tokens: only a hash of each one.
  • On BESNIK, the team gateway logs usage numbers per account (model, tokens, seconds), not the text of your chats.
